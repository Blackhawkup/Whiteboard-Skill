# Writing the narration

The script comes first, on its own, so no images are made before the video's words are decided.

## Word budgets

A voice speaks about 2.5 words a second, but a finished whiteboard film runs closer to 2. The start, the pauses that give the pen time, and the hold on the last frame all add up. Two real films: 76 words made 37 seconds, and 71 words made 35 seconds.

| Length | Words, at most | Drawings |
|---|---|---|
| 30 s | 60 | 6 to 8 |
| 1 min | 120 | 12 to 15 |
| 2 min | 240 | 20 to 26 |
| 5 min | 620 | 45 to 60, in several boards |

If the user gives a hard length, write to this budget from the start, because the script is the one thing that cannot be squeezed later.

## Rules

- **Write nouns you can draw.** "Your car just broke down" draws itself. "Productivity suffers" does not. If a line cannot be drawn, rewrite it until it can.
- **One idea per sentence, and short sentences.** Read it out loud. A sentence you run out of breath on is too long.
- **Use one concrete situation all the way through.** One person with one problem is easier to follow than several examples. If the user asks for an analogy, pick one and stay in it.
- **Say "you".** The viewer is in the story.
- **Open on the situation, not on an introduction.** A standalone video starts with the problem in the first five words. A section inside a longer video starts on its one idea.
- **Set up a contrast when the topic has one.** The same situation told twice, once each way, is the strongest shape a whiteboard has. The final wide shot then shows both side by side.
- **End on one line that could stand alone.** That line becomes the payoff words on the board.
- **Check every fact.** Numbers, quotes and claims get verified before the user sees the script.
- **No stage directions, headings or symbols inside the narration.** `script.md` is read aloud. Separate parts with a blank line.

## Example

> Your car just broke down. So you ask a chatbot what to do. It gives you a list. Call a garage. Book a repair. Find a ride home. Helpful. But you still have to do all of it.
>
> Now, ask an AI agent. It calls the garage, books the repair, orders your ride, and adds it to your calendar. You just go home. A chatbot tells you what to do. An agent does it for you.

Why it works: one situation told twice, every noun is drawable, and the last line is the whole idea.
